UK Garage Website Checklist

15 free checks you can run on your own garage website in about 10 minutes. No sign-up, no tools to install, no technical knowledge needed.

This checklist grew out of a study of 224 UK independent garage, MOT centre and vehicle business websites. A third of them were missing something a customer would expect to find in seconds. These are the same things, written up so you can check your own site right now.

Each item below says what to check, why it matters to a customer standing in a car park with a phone in their hand, and how to check it yourself. None of this is our paid work - it is everything a non-technical owner can test alone. See the full findings for the numbers behind this list.

The 15 checks

  • 1. Tap-to-call phone number

    Why it matters: most people find a garage on their phone, and most of them want to ring you, not fill in a form. If your number is not tappable, some will give up and call the next result instead.

    How to check: open your website on your own mobile phone (not a desktop). Find your phone number and tap it directly. If your phone's dialler opens with your number already entered, it works. If nothing happens, it needs fixing.

  • 2. HTTPS padlock

    Why it matters: browsers warn visitors when a site is "not secure", and some customers will leave rather than continue. Search engines also prefer secure sites.

    How to check: look at the address bar. You should see a small padlock icon and the address should start with https://, not http://. Try typing your address with plain http:// too - it should automatically redirect to the secure version.

  • 3. Opening hours are correct, including bank holidays

    Why it matters: nothing frustrates a customer more than driving to a closed garage. Wrong hours cost you a visit and the trust that goes with it.

    How to check: read your listed hours against what you actually do this week, and check whether bank holiday hours are mentioned anywhere. If they are not, add a line such as "closed on bank holidays" or your actual arrangement.

  • 4. MOT and service information is current

    Why it matters: customers comparing garages often look for what services you offer and whether you do what they need, before they ever call.

    How to check: read your services page as if you were a stranger. Does it say clearly whether you do MOTs, servicing, diagnostics, tyres, or whatever applies to you? Is anything listed that you no longer offer?

  • 5. Mobile page speed

    Why it matters: a slow site loses visitors before they ever see what you offer, especially on a mobile signal in a car park.

    How to check: load your homepage on mobile data (turn off Wi-Fi) and time how long it takes to become usable. If it takes more than a few seconds, or large images visibly load in one by one, it is worth asking your web host or developer to look at it.

  • 6. Google Business Profile is linked

    Why it matters: your Google Business Profile carries your reviews, map pin and quick actions. Linking to it from your site helps customers find and trust both.

    How to check: search your garage name on Google. If a profile panel appears with your reviews and map, check your website links to it (often from a "find us" or "reviews" section, or the map itself).

  • 7. Reviews are visible

    Why it matters: most customers check reviews before booking a garage, especially one they have not used before. Hiding them, even accidentally, costs you trust you have already earned.

    How to check: look for a reviews section, widget or direct link to your Google or Trustpilot reviews. If you have none, decide whether to ask recent customers to leave one.

  • 8. Footer copyright year is up to date

    Why it matters: an old copyright year in the footer is a small thing, but it is one of the first silent signals that a site has been left unattended, which can make a visitor wonder if the garage is still trading.

    How to check: scroll to the bottom of your homepage. If it says a year before the current one and never updates, ask whoever manages your site to fix it, or switch it to update automatically.

  • 9. Contact form actually sends

    Why it matters: a broken contact form loses enquiries silently. You will not know unless you test it, because no error ever reaches you.

    How to check: fill in your own contact form with a test enquiry and submit it. Confirm you receive the email (check your spam folder too) within a few minutes.

  • 10. Address and directions are clear

    Why it matters: some customers drive straight from your website without checking a map app separately, especially regulars who already roughly know where you are.

    How to check: read your address as printed. Is it complete, including postcode? If there is a map, does the pin actually sit on your premises rather than a nearby road?

  • 11. Site works without a Covid-era notice

    Why it matters: an old pandemic notice signals a site that has not been looked at in years, even if everything else still works.

    How to check: read your homepage and any banners for mentions of Covid, coronavirus, or restrictions that no longer apply, and remove them if your current arrangements are different.

  • 12. Images actually load

    Why it matters: broken images (a grey box or a broken-icon symbol) look unprofessional and make a business seem neglected, even if the work behind it is excellent.

    How to check: load every page of your site and look for broken image icons, especially your logo, team or workshop photos. Replace or remove anything broken.

  • 13. The site is readable on a small phone screen

    Why it matters: if text is tiny or a menu will not open on a phone, most visitors simply leave rather than try to zoom and scroll.

    How to check: on your phone, check that text is readable without zooming, buttons are easy to tap, and the menu opens and closes properly.

  • 14. Homepage has been updated recently

    Why it matters: a homepage that has not changed in years can look abandoned, particularly if it mentions old offers, old staff or old prices.

    How to check: read your homepage for anything out of date: an expired offer, a team member who has left, or services you no longer run. Update anything that no longer applies.

  • 15. Someone other than you has tried it

    Why it matters: you know your own site too well to spot what confuses a first-time visitor. A fresh pair of eyes catches things you will not.

    How to check: ask a friend, family member or customer who has never seen your site to find your phone number, opening hours and services, while you watch. Note anywhere they hesitate.
